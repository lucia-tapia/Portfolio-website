Personal Brand

Identity, content strategy and website for my personal brand.

For my degree thesis, I explored personal branding for artists: why it matters, and how a cohesive visual world can communicate an artist’s identity across every touchpoint. I then applied that research to my own practice, developing a personal brand for myself as an aspiring illustrator and designer.

I began by researching how independent illustrators build and engage an audience. From there, I defined my own position, identified the audience I wanted to reach, and considered how I could present myself online in a way that felt authentic to my practice. These insights shaped the visual identity, content strategy, and website, creating a cohesive brand designed to work across every touchpoint.

The website you’re viewing is an extension of that identity, bringing the visual language and strategy together in a fully considered digital experience. The result is a launch-ready brand that communicates a consistent sense of who I am and what I create.

The moodboard was built with references leaning towards editorial typefaces and layouts, handmade and tactile details, everyday photos and, of course, illustration.

A romantic, hand-drawn identity with elegant flourishes. A full logo system with alternative versions ready for packaging and future applications.

The dragonfly also works on its own as the main logo symbol, and together with small hand-drawn ornaments it becomes a pattern for posts, possible packaging and the website.

Burgundy and olive are the main colors, with dark brown and cream as a base. Dusty rose, light olive and butter yellow are for small details, so the palette feels rich but grounded.

A mix of serif and sans serif to create a clear hierarchy: ZT Bros Oskon for titles, Aston Script for accents, EB Garamond for body text and Montserrat for small details.

The social media strategy focuses on documenting the creative world behind the brand: the illustrations and designs, the process and inspiration behind them, and the ideas and lessons that emerge along the way. Each post falls into one of four content pillars, creating variety while maintaining a cohesive visual identity across the feed.

The strategy is built around three posts per week, planned a month in advance. Rather than prioritizing volume, the focus is on consistency and intentionality. Reels introduce the work to new audiences, carousels create opportunities for deeper engagement, single-image posts showcase finished pieces, and Stories capture the more immediate, day-to-day side of the practice.

Content pillars

Process

Drawing or painting process, sketches and materials. Showing the behind the scenes and the value behind each piece.

Finished work

Finished pieces.

Inspiration

Curiosities, books, color combinations, museums, architecture, small everyday details… Things I notice that inspire me or that I find visually appealing.

Everyday

My desk, a café, a walk around the city. A look into the ordinary day, without making it too personal.
