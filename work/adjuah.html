Chief Adjuah

A self-initiated identity concept for jazz artist Christian Scott aTunde Adjuah.

Chief Xian aTunde Adjuah, formerly known as Christian Scott, is a New Orleans trumpeter, composer and instrument designer. He builds his own instruments, coined the term Stretch Music for his genre-blind fusion of jazz, trap and West African rhythm, and is a hereditary Big Chief of the Xodokan Nation, one of New Orleans’ Black Masking Indian traditions.

His work is both ancestral and futuristic. This is a self-initiated identity concept, an exercise in translating such an interesting personality into a complete visual system. It isn’t client work.

Insight

Chief Adjuah doesn’t preserve tradition, he reinvents it by combining things: trap layered over West African percussion, ceremonial suits built from thousands of beads added by hand over a year. His whole practice is fragments, assembled with intent. Once I learned this, I understood the identity had to work the same way: not one single thing, but a system built from parts, each carrying meaning of its own.

The mark

The wordmark evolved in three passes. The first began with the Adjuah trumpet, the horn he designed, abstracted into the J. The second folded his initials, C and A, into the letterforms. The final version introduced the Sankofa bird, the West African symbol of moving forward while looking back, as a central medallion. The custom type adds small notches inspired by Adinkra symbols.

The pattern

The pattern extends the wordmark into a wider visual alphabet, inspired by Adinkra and by the pictorial patches of Black Masking Indian regalia. It works like textile does in his world: repeatable, hand-feeling, and carrying meaning even when read as texture.
